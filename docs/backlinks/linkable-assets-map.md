## 1. Niche definition

WhatIs.site is a **horizontal educational explainer** — 1,615 articles across 25 broad categories (Technology, Science, Math, Finance, Philosophy, History, Psychology, Health, Economics, Law, Arts, Social Sciences, Business, Education, Sports, Music, Food, Language, Engineering, Biology, Geography, Religion, Politics, Marketing, plus Everyday Concepts).

This puts us in the **general-knowledge explainer market**, not a single vertical. Our competitors are:

- Encyclopedic explainers (Wikipedia, Britannica, Encyclopedia.com)
- Vertical explainer leaders that dominate one category each (Investopedia for finance, Verywell for health, Mayo Clinic for medical, Khan Academy for math, TechTarget/WhatIs.com for IT, Wolfram MathWorld for math, ThoughtCo for general)
- "How it works" sites (HowStuffWorks, Vox explainer, NPR explainers)
- AI Overviews / Perplexity / ChatGPT search — increasingly the de-facto top "result"

We will not beat Wikipedia or Investopedia on their best terms head-on. The opportunity is in **fresher data, better-structured FAQ blocks, free tools, and 2026-dated stats roundups** the encyclopedias don't ship.

## 3. Cross-category patterns that earn links

Aggregated from §2 — what consistently earns editorial citations in the explainer niche:

1. **Annual "[Topic] Statistics [Year]"** pages with cited primary sources.
   - Journalists link these on a 30-day-old roundup cycle. HubSpot owns this in marketing/tech.
2. **Free, single-purpose calculators** (mortgage, compound interest, BMI, unit converter, IRR, tip).
   - NerdWallet/Bankrate dominate finance; nobody dominates cross-category.
3. **Plain-English regulation/concept explainers** (what GDPR means, what a yield curve inversion is).
   - Investopedia and Verywell own this — opportunity is in fresher 2026 framing.
4. **"State of [Field]" annual reports** with original data.
   - Hard to do without primary research. SKIP for now.
5. **Glossaries** as link-worthy hubs (the entire site qualifies — needs hub-ification).
6. **Definitive concept entries** with strong FAQ blocks → cited by AI Overviews → cited by humans.
7. **Interactive concept diagrams / decision trees** (rare and link-magnet-worthy).
8. **Timeline / "on this day" data pages**.

## 4. Where WhatIs.site can realistically compete

Given our constraints (no primary research, no outreach, 1,615 existing articles, zero JS by default, Astro static site):

| Asset type | Feasibility | Link potential | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual "X Stats [Year]" pages | High | High | Aggregate cited sources; zero JS works fine |
| Free calculators (math/finance/health) | Medium | High | Need a JS exception per tool, or static lookup tables |
| Deep guides / canonical explainers | High | Medium | Most of our 1,615 articles already are this — needs upgrade, not new |
| Glossary hubs per category | High | Medium | Already have category pages; needs sortable index |
| Interactive concept diagrams | Low | High | Requires JS — pick 1–2 only |
| "State of X" reports | Low | Very High | No primary data — skip |
| Timeline data pages | High | Medium | Static, easy to ship |

**Strategic implication:** the biggest leverage is **upgrading 20–30 existing articles into stats-pages-with-current-data** + **shipping 3–5 free calculators** + **building category glossary hubs**. We do not need new articles for backlink work; we need to convert existing assets into linkable formats.

## 5. AI Overview / Perplexity citation gaps

Citation behavior to bias toward (qualitative — verify with live spot-checks before publishing):

- AI Overviews favor **clean, fact-dense definitions in the first 100 words** with **explicit numerics**.
- Perplexity over-indexes on **sources with structured FAQ schema and clear `<h2>` questions**.
- ChatGPT search favors **freshly dated content** (2025/2026 in title/URL/body).
- Both Perplexity and ChatGPT-search will cite **stats roundups** as their stat source — these are AI-citation gold.

Gaps to exploit:
- Annual stats pages for **niche but high-search-volume topics** (e.g., "remote work statistics 2026", "AI agent adoption statistics 2026") where Wikipedia is too generic and Statista is paywalled.
- 2026-dated versions of **classic explainer terms** that AI Overviews are currently citing 2022/2023 sources for.
